# Employee Onboarding Workflow

## Purpose

This document describes how Harbor Health Services prepares new corporate
employees for their first day and guides them through the first 30 days. The
workflow coordinates Human Resources, hiring managers, Information Technology,
facilities, payroll, and the new employee.

## Team and Volume

- The organization onboards approximately 85 employees each month.
- Two HR coordinators oversee the process across six office locations.
- Most employees are hybrid, while some work remotely in different states.
- New-hire records are normally created two to four weeks before the start date.
- Contractors follow a separate access and equipment process.

## Current Workflow

### 1. New-hire record creation

After an offer is accepted, recruiting enters the employee's name, start date,
manager, department, location, job title, and employment type in the HR system.
An HR coordinator reviews the record and sends the employee forms and policy
acknowledgements. Changes made after the initial entry are communicated by email.

### 2. Department requests

The coordinator creates separate requests for IT access, equipment, facilities,
payroll, and required training. Each department uses a different form or ticket
queue. Managers must identify role-specific applications and equipment, but their
responses are frequently late or incomplete.

### 3. Pre-start tracking

The coordinator tracks completion in a spreadsheet and follows up with each
department. Status terminology differs across teams. A ticket marked complete may
mean equipment was ordered rather than delivered. Coordinators spend several
hours each week reconciling status and sending reminders.

### 4. First-day support

New employees receive a welcome message containing orientation links, benefit
information, technology instructions, and contacts. The message uses a template,
but coordinators manually adjust it for location and employment type. Employees
often ask the same questions about passwords, payroll timing, holidays, and where
to find required training.

### 5. Thirty-day follow-up

HR asks the employee and manager to complete short surveys after the first week
and after 30 days. Coordinators review responses monthly. Recurring delays and
questions are discussed informally, but no consistent report connects onboarding
issues to departments, locations, or employee types.

## Current Challenges

- Information is copied between the HR system, forms, tickets, email, and a spreadsheet.
- Late manager responses delay equipment and application access.
- Status definitions differ across departments and obscure the true readiness date.
- Coordinators send repetitive reminders and answer recurring policy questions.
- Last-minute changes do not always reach every participating department.
- Aggregate survey feedback is reviewed too late to prevent repeated problems.

## Available Data

The organization retains HR-system events, department ticket timestamps, email
templates, onboarding checklists, training completion, and survey responses.
The data includes home addresses, tax information, demographic data, compensation,
and accommodation details that require restricted access. Ticket histories vary
in quality, and department status labels are not standardized.

## Desired Outcomes

HR wants more employees ready on their first day, fewer manual follow-ups, faster
answers to common questions, and earlier visibility into recurring onboarding
delays. Success should be measured by readiness, coordinator effort, response
quality, and employee experience—not simply by the number of automated messages.

## Constraints

- AI must not make hiring, compensation, accommodation, or employment decisions.
- Access recommendations require manager confirmation and existing IT approval.
- Employees must receive authoritative policy sources with any generated answer.
- Sensitive HR data must remain restricted by role and purpose.
- People must be able to reach an HR coordinator when guidance is unclear or personal.
