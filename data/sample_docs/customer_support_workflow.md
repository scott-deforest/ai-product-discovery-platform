# Customer Support Workflow

## Purpose

This document describes the current customer support process for Acme Software. The support team helps customers resolve account, billing, configuration, and product-usage issues.

## Team and Volume

- The support team has 12 support specialists and 2 team leads.
- The team receives approximately 450 requests each week.
- Requests arrive through email, a website form, and in-app chat.
- Normal support hours are Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. Eastern Time.
- The current first-response target is four business hours.

## Current Workflow

### 1. Request intake

A support specialist reviews each new request and manually assigns one of four categories: account access, billing, technical issue, or product question. The specialist also assigns a priority based on the language used by the customer and whether the customer identifies the issue as urgent.

### 2. Initial research

The assigned specialist searches the internal knowledge base, previous support tickets, and product documentation. Information is spread across several systems, so specialists often repeat the same searches. Experienced specialists usually know which terms to use, while newer team members take longer to locate relevant information.

### 3. Customer response

The specialist writes a response using the available evidence. Some common answers have templates, but those templates are not consistently maintained. Specialists frequently rewrite similar explanations for password resets, invoices, and basic configuration questions.

### 4. Escalation

If the specialist cannot resolve a technical issue, the request is escalated to an engineering liaison. Escalations are submitted through a separate form. The form requires a summary, reproduction steps, customer impact, and supporting screenshots. Missing information often causes the liaison to return the escalation for clarification.

### 5. Resolution and closure

After the customer confirms that the issue is resolved, the specialist closes the request and selects a resolution code. Customers receive a satisfaction survey. Team leads review satisfaction scores and a sample of closed requests each month.

## Current Challenges

- Manual categorization is inconsistent, particularly for requests that involve both billing and technical problems.
- Specialists spend significant time searching multiple sources for the same information.
- Repetitive questions still require individually written responses.
- Escalations are delayed when required diagnostic details are missing.
- Knowledge-base articles become outdated after product releases.
- Team leads have limited visibility into emerging issue trends until monthly reporting is complete.

## Available Data

The company retains three years of support-request history, including request text, category, priority, assigned specialist, response timestamps, resolution code, and customer satisfaction score. Personally identifiable customer information is present in some records and must be protected. The knowledge base contains approximately 600 articles, but article ownership and review dates are incomplete.

## Desired Outcomes

The support organization wants to reduce first-response time, improve consistency, make relevant knowledge easier to find, and identify recurring product problems sooner. Any proposed solution should keep a human specialist responsible for customer-facing decisions and should provide evidence for generated recommendations.

## Constraints

- Customer data must remain within approved systems.
- Specialists must be able to review and edit any suggested response before sending it.
- High-priority and security-related requests must always receive human review.
- The initial project should demonstrate measurable value within three months.
