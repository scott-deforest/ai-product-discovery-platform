# Sprint 4 Baseline Evaluation Protocol

## Purpose

Capture the quality of the existing single-analysis pipeline before specialist
output can influence reviewer expectations. These results will become the control
condition for the Sprint 4 comparison.

## Benchmark cases

| Workflow | Saved report | Human quality evaluation | Opportunity decisions | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer support | Complete | Complete — 3.00/5 | 3 of 3 complete | Complete |
| Invoice processing | Complete | Complete — 4.00/5 | 3 of 3 complete | Complete |
| Employee onboarding | Complete | Complete — 4.00/5 | 3 of 3 complete | Complete |

The status table will be updated only from saved application data.

## Locked baseline configuration

- Pipeline: single evidence-grounded opportunity analysis
- Model: `gpt-5.6-sol`
- Prompt version: `opportunity-analysis-v1`
- Source files: the corresponding synthetic workflow in `data/sample_docs/`
- Automatic evaluation: the seven deterministic report checks
- Human evaluation: the five equally weighted Sprint 3 quality criteria

If the configuration changes before all baseline runs are complete, the affected
case must be clearly identified rather than silently treated as comparable.

## Assessment instruction

Use the same instruction for all three workflows:

> Identify evidence-grounded AI opportunities that could create measurable value
> in this workflow. Prioritize bounded validation steps, explicit risks, human
> oversight, and measurable outcomes.

Only the assessment name and source document change between cases.

## Review sequence

For each workflow:

1. Create the assessment using the locked instruction.
2. Upload, extract, chunk, and index the matching synthetic document.
3. Preview the evidence and run the existing single-analysis pipeline once.
4. Confirm the automatic report checks.
5. Complete the five-part human quality evaluation with a rationale for every score.
6. Record one decision and explanatory note for every generated opportunity.
7. Reopen the saved report and verify that the evaluation and decisions persist.
8. Update the benchmark status table and capture the saved results summary.

## Review rules

- Score the report that was generated; do not score what the system might have said.
- Use source citations to judge evidence grounding.
- Treat a score of 3 as useful with revision, not as a failing score.
- Record missing information honestly rather than filling gaps from outside knowledge.
- Do not revise baseline scores after seeing specialist output. If judgment changes,
  preserve the original revision and explain the later revision explicitly.
- Do not choose opportunity decisions merely to create a balanced distribution.

## Completion criteria

The baseline is complete when all three workflows have:

- One saved report generated by the locked pipeline configuration.
- Seven automatic checks recorded.
- One complete human quality evaluation with five written rationales.
- A saved human decision and note for every opportunity.
- Persistence verified by reopening the report.

The baseline is intentionally small and uses one reviewer and synthetic data. It
supports an informed product-development comparison; it does not establish
statistical significance or general model superiority.

## Completed baseline results

All three workflows completed the locked baseline protocol:

| Workflow | Grounding | Specificity | Actionability | Risk awareness | Decision usefulness | Overall |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Customer support | 4 | 2 | 5 | 1 | 3 | 3.00 |
| Invoice processing | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4.00 |
| Employee onboarding | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4.00 |
| **Average** | **4.00** | **3.33** | **4.33** | **3.00** | **3.67** | **3.67** |

- Three saved reports passed all seven automatic checks.
- Nine opportunities received saved human decisions.
- Four opportunities advanced to validation.
- Four opportunities need more evidence.
- One opportunity was deferred.
- No opportunity was rejected outright.

### Observed strengths

- Evidence grounding was consistently strong across all three workflows.
- Recommendations generally proposed bounded validation rather than autonomous action.
- Reports were actionable enough to support explicit advance, investigate, or defer decisions.
- Financial and employment workflows included concrete human-review boundaries and safeguards.

### Observed weaknesses

- Specificity and risk analysis were inconsistent, especially in the earlier customer-support report.
- Every workflow lacked important operational baselines such as time, cost, error, volume, or impact.
- Several opportunities depended on unconfirmed source quality, integration access, ownership, or governance rules.
- The reports identified missing evidence but did not consistently organize it into a prioritized validation plan.

### Sprint 4 comparison hypotheses

The specialist pipeline should be retained only if it can measurably:

1. Improve specificity by tying recommendations more closely to workflow details,
   data readiness, operating ownership, and pilot boundaries.
2. Improve risk awareness without producing generic risk lists or unnecessary caution.
3. Convert missing information into a clearer, prioritized validation plan.
4. Improve decision usefulness enough to justify additional model calls, latency,
   operational complexity, and failure modes.

These results establish a product-development baseline, not a scientific benchmark.
The sample contains three synthetic workflows reviewed by one person, and model
outputs may vary between runs.
